Frank Rosaly Assembles the Chicago–Amsterdam REFLEX Quartet at the Bimhuis

One night at the Bimhuis on 1 October: the Puerto Rican drummer at the head of a four-piece with Jeff Parker, Rob Frye and Ibelisse Guardia Ferragutti, commissioned by BIMHUIS Productions and built around the MESTIZX material. For most of the room, though, this will be a Jeff Parker night — the drummer’s commission, the guitarist’s crowd.

Bimhuis, Piet Heinkade 3, Amsterdam — home of the REFLEX commission on 1 October. Photo via Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 3.0.

On Thursday 1 October 2026, 20:30, Frank Rosaly takes over the main Bimhuis Zaal at the head of a Chicago–Amsterdam quartet: Jeff Parker on guitar and electronics, Rob Frye on flutes, tenor saxophone, EWI and electronics, and Ibelisse Guardia Ferragutti on synths, voice and percussion. It is a commissioned edition of BIMHUIS Productions’ REFLEX series, which asks its makers to “reflect on the world and contemporary society.” On paper it is a supergroup date. In practice it is the amplified, four-piece staging of the MESTIZX repertoire — the Rosaly and Guardia Ferragutti album released by Chicago’s International Anthem in May 2024 — with the record’s two closest studio collaborators, Parker and Frye, brought into the live band.

The neatest way to describe Rosaly is with the paragraph he almost never gets: Phoenix-born (1974) to Puerto Rican parents, fifteen years in the Chicago improvised-music orbit that Chicago Reader named him Best Jazz Musician of 2013 out of, then a 2016 move to Amsterdam that he has since made his working base — co-founding the Molk Factory label and performance space, appearing on more than 150 recordings, and building a body of work that increasingly reads as a single decades-long argument about identity, ritual and the drum. The 1 October Bimhuis date is the fullest live translation of that argument he has brought to Amsterdam so far.

Who Frank Rosaly is

Rosaly’s Chicago decade put him in ensembles with, among many others, Bobby Bradford, Thurston Moore, Nels Cline, Joshua Abrams’ Natural Information Society, Colin Stetson, Ben LaMar Gay and the late Jaimie Branch — a run that placed him inside almost every consequential post-AACM improvised-music circuit Chicago produced in the 2000s and 2010s. When he relocated to Amsterdam in 2016, he brought that network with him, and it has kept extending: through Molk Factory, through recurring guest weeks at Bimhuis, and through the two ensembles he leads with Guardia Ferragutti — Mestizx and Ruidoscuro.

The two projects share DNA. Both draw on Rosaly’s late-arriving investigation of his own Puerto Rican and diasporic Latin-American inheritance, and both are built around Guardia Ferragutti’s Bolivian voice and multi-instrumentalism. What separates them is scale and vocabulary: Mestizx is the duo statement, released on Chicago’s International Anthem in May 2024 and described by the label as “a deeply personal meditation on decolonization and the defiant power of ritual and protest”; Ruidoscuro is the electrified sextet that expands it. The Bimhuis quartet on 1 October is a third variation, sitting between the two.

The quartet, decoded

The bill reads Rosaly first. The room fills for Parker.

Jeff Parker. Chicago-raised, Los Angeles-based, guitarist for Tortoise since the mid-1990s — his first full record with them, 1996’s Millions Now Living Will Never Die, is arguably the American post-rock foundational text — and, twenty-eight years on, one of the most-cited guitarists in contemporary jazz. His run of leader and quartet records for Chicago’s International Anthem — 2016’s The New Breed, 2020’s Suite for Max Brown (the record most likely to have got a new listener into him), the 2022 live-quartet Mondays at The Enfield Tennis Academy, and 2024’s The Way Out of Easy — reset what a jazz-adjacent guitar record could sound like on a small independent label, and made him the guitarist a critic cites when explaining what improvised music with a rhythm section is doing in the mid-2020s. He has played on Rosaly-adjacent Chicago sessions for two decades and on the MESTIZX album itself. Hearing him at the head of a Rosaly-led band, on this repertoire, is a different proposition to hearing him lead his own.

Tortoise, live in Santiago de Compostela. Jeff Parker on the right; the band Parker has been part of since 1996. Photo by Bene Rioó via Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 3.0.

Rob Frye. The most left-field name on the bill and, for anyone who knows the record, the least surprising. Frye is one third of Bitchin’ Bajas — the Chicago drone/minimalist trio whose 2015 Epic Jams collaboration with Bonnie ‘Prince’ Billy is one of the twenty-first century’s better-loved slow records — and he played flute and reeds on the MESTIZX album sessions at International Anthem’s Chicago studios. He is also a member of Mike Reed’s The Separatist Party. Expect long tones, EWI-processed sax, and the kind of textural low-end that a room like the Bimhuis Zaal was designed for.

Ibelisse Guardia Ferragutti. Bolivian-born, Amsterdam-based singer, arranger and theatre-maker whose vocal range on MESTIZX — moving between Andean folk phrasing, spoken word, and multi-tracked electronic processing — is the album’s emotional through-line. On stage she moves between synths, percussion and voice, often within the same passage. On 1 October she is the reason the quartet doesn’t sound like “four Americans plus a singer”.

Whether this specific four-piece has ever played together on stage before this night is unclear from public records. What is clear is that the Bimhuis is presenting it as a REFLEX commission, not a tour date — meaning the programme has been shaped for this room, this audience and this evening.

Why count this night

Rosaly is 51. He has been in Amsterdam for a decade — long enough that he shows up in the Bimhuis annual programming as a resident rather than a visitor. The Chicago players he is bringing back for this REFLEX date — Parker and Frye, both of whom he has worked with for the better part of twenty years — are touring less than they did five years ago, for the ordinary reasons of career and geography. The specific overlap of these four musicians in this room, on this date, on this repertoire, is not one that repeats itself on demand. Bimhuis is not selling the night as historic. In retrospect it will read that way.

There is also a scene-level point. Amsterdam’s improvised-music infrastructure — Bimhuis, Splendor, OT301, Zaal 100, the Muziekgebouw’s occasional experimental slots — supports a small number of artists at Rosaly’s level of visibility. When one of them uses a REFLEX commission to fly in his closest American collaborators and stage the record with the Amsterdam partner who co-wrote it, that is the sort of one-off the Bimhuis calendar is built to make possible, and the sort that a serious listening city should turn up for.

The complement: 28 August at the Concertgebouw

Het Concertgebouw, Van Baerlestraat, Amsterdam — Choir Hall hosts the Rosaly/Guardia Ferragutti duo on 28 August. Photo by yisris via Wikimedia Commons, CC BY 2.0.

Five weeks before the Bimhuis date, on Friday 28 August 2026, 19:30, Rosaly and Guardia Ferragutti bring the same MESTIZX material to the Concertgebouw’s Choir Hall, but stripped to duo — voice and drums, minimal electronics, no Parker, no Frye. It is a Basement Sessions concert, part of the house’s SummerConcerts strand, and at €19 (plus a €5 order fee) one of the cheapest doors the Concertgebouw offers all year. Different night, different bill, different room — same record at its intimate core. If you want to hear MESTIZX in the two most opposite institutional settings Amsterdam can offer, five weeks apart, this is the pairing. Tickets and details on the Concertgebouw page.

A primer, if you’re new to Jeff Parker

You don’t need this section if Tortoise, International Anthem, or Suite for Max Brown already mean something to you. If they don’t, here is a ninety-minute walk through Parker’s world: start with Tortoise’s 1996 post-rock foundational text for the Chicago context he arrived from; jump to his own 2012 Delmark solo record for the guitar language undiluted; then two International Anthem records — the 2016 debut and the 2020 breakthrough — that reset what a jazz-adjacent band could sound like on a small independent label; finish with a track from the 2024 live-in-Los Angeles record to hear him now. In order, one listen. You’ll know by the end whether Rosaly’s quartet at the Bimhuis is a night for you. The four Chicago and Los Angeles records are in the cards below.

The Amsterdam–Chicago axis, 25 years in

Rosaly’s Bimhuis night does not sit alone in the season, and it does not sit alone in the history. In June 1998, at Konkurrent’s Koeienverhuur studio in Amsterdam, The Ex and Tortoise recorded In the Fishtank 5 — six tracks, released in 1999 on Konkurrent, that fused The Ex’s punk-informed improvisation with Tortoise’s Chicago post-rock architecture. The Ex brought guitarist Andy Moor (who had joined the band in 1990 after moving from Scotland to the Netherlands); Tortoise brought Dan Bitney, John Herndon, John McEntire, Douglas McCombs and Jeff Parker, with Rob Mazurek on cornet. It was one of the first hard documents of a Konkurrent-orbit Amsterdam–Chicago traffic that has run in both directions ever since.

Rosaly is the current Chicago node of that traffic. He came up through the Chicago improvised-music scene of the 2000s (Ken Vandermark, Jason Adasiewicz, Nicole Mitchell), moved to Amsterdam in the mid-2010s, and now works both sides of the Atlantic without switching accent. In June 2022 he sat behind the drums at Bimhuis with Moor and Puglian baritone saxophonist Giuseppe Doronzo for a Red Light Jazz Festival commission — the set that Clean Feed released in 2024 as Futuro Ancestrale. That record is the direct throughline from Fishtank 5 to Rosaly’s 2026 REFLEX date.

Which is why autumn 2026 at Bimhuis reads as a pair. Rosaly opens the run tonight with Evan Parker; Doronzo closes it on 25 October with Koper8, on the same stage where Futuro Ancestrale was cut — FOMOdam feature here. Neither night happens in a vacuum.
