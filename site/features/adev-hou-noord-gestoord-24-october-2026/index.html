Hou Noord Gestoord — ADEV Walks Through Noord for Its Twelfth Edition

Saturday 24 October 2026, 13:00, Amsterdam Noord. A participant's notes on twelve years of Amsterdam Danst Ergens Voor, from De Valreep to the Slibvelden.

Preview draft. Unlisted URL shared with ADEV for review before publication. Not indexed, not linked from FOMOdam. Playlist companion and any imagery to be added once agreed.

I have walked with ADEV before. This year the route runs through Noord.

What ADEV is

ADEV stands for Amsterdam Danst Ergens Voor — Amsterdam Dances For Something. The format is straightforward: a soundsystem on wheels, a permitted route through the city, and a set of demands that circle back, edition after edition, to the same theme — space for the alternative city. On Saturday 24 October 2026 the march is called Hou Noord Gestoord — Keep Noord Weird — and the starting point will be announced closer to the day.

How it began

The first ADEV, in the summer of 2013, was a defence of De Valreep, the squatted social centre on Polderweg in Oost. That building held out for another eleven months before the eviction on 17 June 2014, which ended one of the last large squats east of the Amstel. ADEV kept going after De Valreep closed. Its subsequent editions have tracked, in a rough chronology, the map of what Amsterdam was losing: warehouses, self-organised venues, cheap live–work buildings, the small ecology that made scenes possible.

ADM and the move to the Slibvelden

When the ADM community was evicted from its Westhaven grounds in January 2019 after twenty-two years, the search for a next site became the through-line of ADEV's mid-decade editions. That search eventually landed at the Slibvelden Noord, the former sludge fields opposite the NDSM, where a portion of the former ADM community rebuilt. The Slibvelden is a partial answer, not a substitute — smaller footprint, different constraints — but it exists, and it is one of the few concrete outcomes visible in ADEV's twelve-year run.

The 2022 edition

Not every edition has ended well. The 2022 march broke down on the Vondelstraat and continued into the Constantijn Huygensstraat, where plainclothes officers moved into the crowd and Mobiele Eenheid units deployed. Several participants were hospitalised. There is a version of that day published by the police and a version that circulated through the participants afterwards; the two do not fully reconcile. Whatever the sequence, the day left a residue that shaped how later editions were organised — more visible marshalling, clearer route agreements, and, on the participant side, a more careful reading of who is standing near you.

And 2025

Last year's edition, at Java-eiland, was smaller by design. A micro-demo, called and dispersed by the organisers themselves at 17:00. The choice not to press further that day was, in its own way, a statement about the space that authorised demonstration still occupies in Amsterdam in 2025.

Why Noord in 2026

Noord is the borough where the pressures ADEV names are currently most visible in real time. The former industrial strip along the IJ has moved through the standard sequence — artist tenants, festival programming, tower cranes — quickly enough that the earlier stages are still legible in the current landscape. Buiksloterham, Overhoeks, NDSM: three names on a short list of neighbourhoods that were, ten years ago, understood as places the city had set aside for the informal, and are now something else. Hou Noord Gestoord is not a nostalgia call — the borough is not gone, and the vrijplaatsen that remain, the Slibvelden included, are still doing the work — but it is a request, in public, to slow one particular clock.

On the day itself

ADEV is a legal demonstration: agreed route, organiser marshals, official end point. At past editions I’ve felt less protected than closely watched — a policing presence that scales with the crowd. Legal, loud, and never alone on the street.
