Giuseppe Doronzo brings Koper8 to Bimhuis: an undersea-cable premiere for Europe

The most interesting baritone saxophonist under 40 in Amsterdam isn’t Dutch. He’s from Barletta, on the Adriatic coast of Puglia, and on 25 October he’ll be at Bimhuis to premiere a project he built with a guitarist from Athens and two Brooklyn heavyweights.

Bimhuis, Piet Heinkade 3, Amsterdam — home of the Koper8 European premiere on 25 October. Photo via Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 3.0.

The Italy–Amsterdam bridge

Giuseppe Doronzo has lived long enough in the Netherlands that the Dutch press has stopped calling him “the Italian.” Trouw now just says he “holds a brilliant argument for the baritone sax as a leading instrument.” Jazzism, more expansively, called him “one of the most subtly articulate baritone saxophone players in the world.” The paperwork is Barlettan. The vocabulary is Amsterdam impro.

This is the story FOMOdam keeps circling: the way a certain kind of Italian musician arrives in the Lowlands with classical training, absorbs the improvised-music discipline that Amsterdam has spent fifty years refining around Bimhuis and Zaal 100, and then loops back out to Puglia, or Groningen, or Brooklyn, and becomes a bridge instead of an emigrant. Doronzo is one of the sharpest examples of that pattern working. On 25 October, at 20:30, he brings the newest version of it into the Bimhuis Zaal.

Who is Giuseppe Doronzo

Born in 1987 in Barletta, Doronzo did the full classical apprenticeship first: saxophone at the “N. Piccinni” Conservatory in Bari (graduating in 2009), then a Master’s in classical-contemporary saxophone at the “N. Rota” Conservatory in Monopoli in 2011. He moved to the Netherlands in 2012 to study jazz and composition at the Prins Claus Conservatory in Groningen — among his teachers, Michael Moore. He graduated cum laude in 2015 and settled in Amsterdam.

The sound is easier to describe than to place. Doronzo focuses his research on the baritone saxophone, pushing it as a lead voice rather than a bottom-end colourist, and he explicitly weaves three strands: contemporary classical technique (multiphonics, extended articulations), free improvisation in the Dutch/European tradition (Moore, Han Bennink, Wilbert de Joode all appear in his orbit), and non-Western material — makam and microtonal work, and reed and flute instruments including mizmar and bansuri. On the Koper8 date he’s also credited with Iranian bagpipe alongside baritone.

The recognition has been steady rather than viral. He was named “Music Revelation 2018” by the international jazz-critic poll El Intruso (through All About Jazz’s Karl Ackermann). In 2022 he won a Buma Music in Motion Award for best original composition for a short film (the Roman Ermolaev-directed GOYA, which shares its title with Doronzo’s 2018 solo album). His current working groups include the AVA Trio (with bassist Esat Ekincioglu and Puglian percussionist Pino Basile), the duo Aterraterr with Basile, the quintet Falga, the Michael Moore Bigtet, All Ellington, Raoul van der Weide’s Xavier Pamplona Ensemble, and now Koper8. He is a Drake Mouthpieces and Wood Wind Design artist. He teaches. He performs in Bimhuis regularly enough that his name has stopped being news there — which is precisely when a musician stops being a visitor and starts being infrastructure.

The Amsterdam–Chicago axis, 25 years in

In June 1998, at Konkurrent’s Koeienverhuur studio in Amsterdam, The Ex and Tortoise spent two days recording In the Fishtank 5 — six tracks, released in 1999 on Konkurrent, that welded The Ex’s punk-informed improvisation to Tortoise’s Chicago post-rock architecture. The Ex brought Terrie, Katrin, Luc, GW Sok, and guitarist Andy Moor, who had joined the band in 1990 after moving to the Netherlands. Tortoise brought Dan Bitney, John Herndon, John McEntire, Douglas McCombs, and guitarist Jeff Parker (with Rob Mazurek on cornet).

That session set a template the Konkurrent orbit has been extending ever since: North American improvisers looping through Amsterdam not as tourists but as collaborators, and Amsterdam-based players heading back the other way. Andy Moor is one of the through-lines. Twenty-six years after Fishtank 5, he is on Futuro Ancestrale (Clean Feed, 2024) with Doronzo and Chicago-trained drummer Frank Rosaly — part of the same International Anthem-orbit Chicago cohort as cornettist Ben LaMar Gay and Jeff Parker — recorded live at Bimhuis in June 2022 as a Red Light Jazz Festival commission.

Which is what makes autumn 2026 at Bimhuis worth watching as a pair, not two isolated nights. Rosaly opens the run on 1 October with his REFLEX quartet and Evan Parker (FOMOdam feature); Doronzo closes it on 25 October with Koper8, on the same stage where Futuro Ancestrale was cut five weeks earlier. Neither night happens in a vacuum.

The Koper8 project

Koper is Dutch for copper (and, by extension, brass). Koper8 — pronounced roughly “koper-acht” — is a new quartet co-founded by Doronzo and Athens-based guitarist and pedal-builder Jannis Anastasakis. The concept, as the band frames it, is our telecommunications infrastructure: we call it wireless, but almost all of it still runs along the ocean floor through undersea cables. Koper8 turns that hidden network into a musical event — improvisation as live broadcast, with the noise deliberately preserved: the crackle, the interference, the pressure of ocean on the line. Baritone and tenor saxophones are pushed through handmade electronic effects, drums modulate the signal, and deep drones fold into psychedelic textures.

The self-titled debut album was recorded at Figure 8 Studios in Brooklyn and comes out this autumn on Defkaz Records. The Bimhuis show is billed as the band’s first European appearance and doubles as the album’s release concert. It is programmed under the venue’s Impro Focus strand.

The quartet, decoded

Doronzo is the pivot. Anastasakis is the co-founder. But the casting of the two Americans is what makes this a curatorial statement rather than just a good gig.

Ingrid Laubrock (tenor sax) is the reference point for the current New York avant-jazz saxophone school. Born in Stadtlohn, Germany in 1970, based in London through the 1990s and 2000s, and in Brooklyn since 2009, she leads Anti-House, Sleepthief, Ubatuba and her own septet, and she is a longtime member of the Wet Ink Ensemble. Bringing Laubrock in signals that Doronzo isn’t proposing a Mediterranean-flavoured set with two American sidemen — he’s proposing two front-line saxophones in genuine dialogue, one baritone/Puglia, one tenor/Brooklyn.

Tom Rainey (drums), born 1957 in Santa Barbara, is Laubrock’s most frequent collaborator (they are a couple and a working duo) and a foundational Brooklyn improvised-music drummer whose CV runs through Tim Berne, Mark Helias, Kris Davis and Mary Halvorson. The Tom Rainey Trio with Laubrock and Halvorson (albums including Pool School and Hotel Grief on Intakt) is one of the touchstone small-group projects of the last fifteen years. Rainey doesn’t keep time so much as modulate it — which is exactly what a band whose organising metaphor is “signal transmission with the noise left in” needs behind the drums.

Jannis Anastasakis (guitar and electronics), the co-composer with Doronzo, is Athens’s most idiosyncratic guitarist. He founded JAM Pedals (the boutique effects company), has been a working duo partner of the great ECM singer Savina Yannatou since 2017, and has performed with Norwegian trumpeter Arve Henriksen among others. He is the reason “handmade electronic effects” is part of the Koper8 pitch and not a decorative phrase.

Put the four together and you get a band that reads on paper like a summit and, on the description, like a physics experiment. Two saxophones that can either lock or shear; a guitarist whose pedalboard is his own workshop; a drummer who lives in the space between pulse and cloud. First European date. Album out. Bimhuis Zaal.

Why this night counts

Impro programming at Bimhuis routinely brings in the New York names — Rainey and Laubrock in particular have appeared in Amsterdam many times in different formations. What is less common is a Bimhuis night where the New Yorkers arrive as guests of an Amsterdam project, curated and led from here. That is the FOMOdam angle: this is Doronzo (Barletta / Amsterdam) and Anastasakis (Athens) framing the American musicians, not the other way around. The Mediterranean impro scene doing the inviting. It’s also a rare chance to hear Doronzo in a setting closer to the noisy, electronic, cross-continental edge of his practice than the more folk-leaning AVA Trio or the solo GOYA world.
