SevenEight comes home: the Classic House Party, with Nakadia in the garden

Amsterdam’s most closely-guarded techno party is the one you get invited to via WhatsApp — and on Saturday 29 August, the SevenEight collective opens its own garden to Nakadia, a full family of residents, and a room-full of patrons and party goers ready to celebrate. Regular tickets are sold out, the official resale sits on TicketSwap.

On Saturday 29 August 2026, from 12:00 to 22:30, SevenEight — the Amsterdam techno collective that started as a house party and soon became a new FOMO — opens its home for The Classic House Party. It is the collective’s only summer 2026 event, a full-day with special guest Nakadia — the Thai-born Berlin techno producer, Carl Cox Intec signee and RESISTANCE resident — plus the residents family and two new arrivals on the bill. This is, as they have been saying on Instagram all summer, “the event that defines who we are.”

Who SevenEight are

The name is a birth-certificate joke that stuck. Two friends, born on 7 and 8 April, decided in a difficult year to throw a joint birthday at home. That was 2024. The party ran long, ended well, and the room asked when the next one was. They organised another. And another. By the time they thought to name the thing, the dates — 7 and 8 — had already named it for them.

Two years on, SevenEight is a small ecosystem: a WhatsApp community you join by invite, a residents’ family, a growing back-office team, a SoundCloud page, and, since January 2026, an official Stichting — the Dutch non-profit foundation status the collective took to formalise what had already been happening in practice. Past guests read like a masterclass in whom you can quietly book if you know what you are doing: Secret Cinema, the Dutch techno lifer who founded Gem Records and produced the first Meng Syndicate records in 1991; Answer Code Request, Patrick Gräser, the Berghain resident on Ostgut Ton; Carmen Lisa and Sole Dosi from the Dutch peak-time and hypnotic-techno circuit. SevenEight’s booking logic, if you had to name it, is community first, headline second — but with a headline that pays its way.

The Classic House Party is a homecoming

Every SevenEight event to date has borrowed a room: Next Door, De Fik Garden, Waterhouse Studios for PULSE, Toekomstmuziek for two joint nights with the Sonant collective. The Classic House Party is the collective’s return to the private residence in Amsterdam where the whole thing started — the same garden and living room that hosted the original 7/8 April birthday two summers ago, now programmed as a proper one-day festival with a licensed venue setup, a sound rig and a scheduled 12:00–22:30 window. That is the symbolic weight. The programme itself is arranged the way SevenEight always arranges its programme: the day opens groovy, deep house and tech house through the afternoon, and moves progressively into hypnotic, driving techno as the sun drops. Nakadia lands in the middle of that arc. The residents open the day and hold its tails. And because the room is a house — the actual house — the door stays under community-only rules: no lists, no last-minute walkups, resale via TicketSwap only. If you have a ticket, you were meant to be there.

Nakadia in the garden

Born Seephrai Mungphanklang on 30 December 1980 in Khon Buri, Thailand as the youngest daughter of a farmer, Nakadia is the outlier profile in modern techno. She quit school as a teenager, worked factory shifts near Bangkok, taught herself English at an internet café, went to Germany in 2002 on a modelling job, walked into a Frankfurt club, and heard techno for the first time. She moved to Koh Samui in 2003, learned to DJ, and has since spent twenty-plus years touring roughly ninety countries with a percussive, punchy, groove-first sound that has landed her on Carl Cox’s Intec Digital, on Filth on Acid, Codex, Kraftek, Get Physical, IAMT, and — most recently — on Hydrozoa (December 2024’s We Are One) and Kuukou (February 2026’s Wild Girl). She is a RESISTANCE resident, a Tomorrowland and Awakenings regular, and — since 2025 — the author of her own autobiography, Positive Energy: Becoming Nakadia, co-written with her manager Sebastian Lehmann. What she brings to the SevenEight garden is the opposite of a big-room headline: a heads-down, groove-driven set from a DJ whose reputation is built on reading rooms the size of festival stages and the size of somebody’s living room, and calibrating to both. The Amsterdam house is the smaller end of her 2026 diary. That is the point.

The residents family

SevenEight’s booking always starts from the residents, and the 29 August bill is the whole family plus two new arrivals.

The peak-time wing is where the day builds: Robert De Neer and Rebrånded (B2B), trailed all summer; Caelestis, whose sets have anchored the residents’ shows through the collective’s history; Vale.T, who played the collective’s PULSE night at Waterhouse Studios and whose “techno battle” Instagram bracket is the running house-style joke. Expect these four to run the room through the sunset hours.

The groove-and-tech-house wing takes the daylight: Tiki Masawa, the newest resident, added earlier this year after opening slots at Toekomstmuziek and De Fik Garden, and MoodaDJ, both known for dancefloor-driven, warm selections that keep the buffet-and-sunshine mood going.

The new additions — Phil-O and Octave Disorder — are the reason to arrive at 12:00. Phil-O is Italian, Amsterdam-based, founder of Wanderlast, a groove-driven house DJ whose sets have been supported by Jamie Jones, Joseph Capriati, Chase West and Cristina Lazic. He has played ADE, Circolo degli Illuminati in Rome, Papagayo in Tenerife. Octave Disorder is opening live — the only live set on the bill. Between them they open the day: the buffet is out, the garden is quiet, and the room is finding its shape.

The Italian buffet

An unusual detail, for a techno event: a rich Italian buffet, communally set out and running throughout the day. It reads as a Sunday-lunch decision made on a Friday, and it tells you almost everything about what SevenEight is trying to be. Most Amsterdam techno rooms sell you a €7 bottle of water and a wristband. This one feeds you at midday, and expects you to still be there — eating, dancing, walking through the garden — at 22:00. Community first, event second. The buffet is the thesis statement.

If you’re new to Nakadia

If Intec, Filth on Acid or IAMT already ring a bell, skip. If not: forty-five minutes across four records that map her arc from the Ballroom Records years into her current label present. Start with Positive Energy (EP) — the 2020 IAMT release whose title she borrowed back for the autobiography — for the working sound. Acid Storm on Carl Cox’s Intec Digital tells you what got Cox on the phone in the first place. Jump to We Are One on Hydrozoa (December 2024) for where she is now on the label circuit. Finish with Wild Girl on Kuukou (February 2026) — the freshest record on the shelf. The four cards are below.
